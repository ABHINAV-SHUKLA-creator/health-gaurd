HealthGuard

Explainable heart-disease risk screening. Two models, and every result comes with its reasons.

Educational project. Not a medical device and not medical advice. If you have symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath or fainting, contact a doctor or emergency services.

Clinical check

Seven answers you could get from a recent checkup. Trained on about 920 patients from four hospitals (UCI Heart Disease data).

Lifestyle check

Eight answers about you and your health history. Trained on about 230,000 US survey responses (BRFSS 2015).

How to read a result

  • Your answers are matched to a risk group: lower, moderate or higher.
  • For each group we show how many out of 100 people in our data actually had (or reported) heart disease. These rates were measured on people the model was not trained on.
  • The list of reasons shows which answers raised or lowered your result, and how strongly.
  • Clinical check: based on patients referred for heart tests, so the rates are much higher than in the general population.
  • Lifestyle check: based on 2015 US survey answers; it describes who reported heart disease, not who will develop it.

Limitations

  • Small, old and narrow data: the clinical data is from the 1980s and about 920 patients; the lifestyle data is US self-reported survey data from 2015.
  • Neither model has been tested on people outside these datasets.
  • Fewer than a handful of non-disease patients exist at one hospital (Switzerland), so results there are unstable.
  • Self-reported answers can be wrong, and the model cannot see anything you do not enter.
  • Not validated for any real medical decision.

Built as a learning project with scikit-learn, SHAP and FastAPI. Data: UCI Heart Disease (CC BY 4.0) and CDC BRFSS 2015 (via Kaggle).
